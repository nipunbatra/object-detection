# Object detection lecture: notes for future sessions

- One deck: `index.html` + `content.js` (slides) + `widgets.js` (figure kit and the three live figures) + `lecture.css`, on the shared engine in `shared/` (copied from `generative/lectures/shared`; `deck.js` takes the page title from `meta.series` and drops "optional extension" from Appendix kickers).
- Route: Part 1 why detect (six applications, three hard cases), 2 label to list, 3 boxes and IoU, 4 one box then many, 5 a grid of cells (Andrew Ng's YOLO), 6 NMS, 7 three grids, 8 measuring, wrap-up; then Appendix A (DETR, slides made with `A_()`, `optional`) and Appendix B (`O()`). Optional slides show only with `?all`, in `?reading` and in the PDFs.
- Every displayed number comes from `data/science.json`. Change a number by changing `scripts/science.py` (which asserts what the slides claim), never by typing it into `content.js`. Order: `run_models.py` → `science.py` → `build_notebook.py --run` → `verify.mjs` → `density.mjs` → `contact_sheets.py --all` → `export.mjs`.
- Colours mean something: teal = annotation (made by people), purple = a model's output. The cells, parcel and crack of Part 1 have no COCO class; their boxes are ours (`data/applications.json`) and are always teal.
- Running example: `figures/cat-dog.jpg` (960 × 640). Every photo's sides are multiples of 32 so YOLOv8 runs without resizing and grid cells are photo pixels. Dog GT (407, 83, 768, 588); worked cell (row 10, col 18) at stride 32. Part 1's missed bird (`cat-dog-bird.jpg`, best box IoU 0.913, score 0.098) returns in Part 8.
- Slide helpers: `S_(id, section, title, {builds, body, lines, ask, expected, ...})` with one story line, one question and one expected answer per build; `partBreak(k, …)` opens a part with its route strip, `recap(k, …)` closes it with short numbered lines and a story line that says what comes next. Caveats, sources and teacher cues go in `point`/notes, not on the slide.
- Keep slides light (Nipun’s request, November 2026): the slide body never repeats the story line (the body holds the figure, the numbers and short labels; the line tells the story); split a slide that makes two moves; story lines at most 30 words; a reveal at most 100 words, aiming under 70, not counting code or the part strip; code blocks at most 12 lines. `node scripts/density.mjs` checks the limits and lists the slides worth a look. The Part 1 jobs show the question first, then swap it for the answer with `only()`.
- Write plain sentences (a humanizer pass, November 2026): no slogan fragments such as “Two dogs, two boxes.” or “Fixed size in, any size out.”, no forced groups of three, no “X, not Y” endings, no decorative bold, and no em or en dashes (write “rows 9 to 11”, “2016 and 2017”). Curly quotes and apostrophes are the house style. Body and story line must not paraphrase each other.
- Parts 4 and 5 follow Andrew Ng's Course 4, Week 3 videos and stay simple (Nipun, November 2026: “teach simply, show shapes, show examples”). Part 4 is his localiser: box head, the record with “?”, his two-case loss, two animals hedging. Part 5.1: one window slid a step at a time, every place it goes, a bigger window, then what is wrong with sliding windows (`sliding-cons`, VGG16 timings and its surest window from `S.convwin`). Nipun asked to skip Ng's convolution tricks on the main route: `fc-conv`, `conv-windows` and `conv-photo` are Appendix B slides. 5.2 and 5.3 are two separate parts, as he asked: ground truth first, then prediction. 5.2, the ground truth: YOLO's name and the 3 × 2 grid as a consequence of choosing 320-pixel cells (`S.ng3`), a person draws the boxes (`gt-annotate`), the grid over them and each centre's cell (`centre-cell`, one object at a time, no “answers for” wording), the cat's and dog's boxes in their cells (`cellZoom`), the whole table, the drag-a-box widget (`target-play`), `build_target` in code (`target-code`). 5.3, the prediction: a small CNN's 2 × 3 × 7 output (`grid-structure`, the real toy architecture), the two tables lined up cell by cell with the untrained network's real numbers (`align-loss`), training on this one photo (`train-toy`; `S.toy` from `run_models.py toy`), the loss in code (`loss-grid`), then crowds: `two-in-a-cell` is the `crowd-grid` widget (choose 3 × 2 to 40 × 22; red cells hold two or more centres; `S.crowd_cells.by_grid`), and two boxes per cell on `figures/person-car.jpg` (`two-boxes`, then `anchor-person` and `anchor-car`, which match each object to the tall or wide template by shape IoU with every area written out; `S.pc`). 5.4, every number worked out (Nipun asked how 588 and 336 arise): `fine-grid` fills a table row per click (centre, ÷ 32, round down, inside the cell, size), `decode` and `decode-box` read YOLOv8n's record back into pixels (corner, centre, size, edges; the rounded steps must stay within 1 px of the real box), `scores-80` (what a cell writes: four box numbers and 80 class scores as bars, each a sigmoid, one per COCO class, with the dog and a wall cell; `S.grid.top` holds every cell's eight highest scores from `run_models.py scores32`), the cell picker (`cell-inspector`, buttons for `S.grid.samples`, all 80 scores as bars, no heat map), and `candidates`: up to 600 boxes, the 0.25 score filter on the five sample cells in three lines of code, 30 pass plus one from the 16-pixel grid; it says plainly that this is a score filter and NMS comes in Part 6. Then the whole algorithm (`yolo-steps`). Slide text does not name Ng (Nipun: no need to say who uses what); credit stays in the speaker notes.
- Part 8 (rebuilt again on 4 Nov 2026; Nipun: the real-data metrics slides “complicate things”, use an educational example): a generated park photo with five dogs and eight made-up boxes (`data/dog-park.json`, `S.teach`; science.py asserts the intended verdicts: TP, TP, the bag, TP, the bench, a box too big, TP, a bush; dog E missed; no duplicates, since NMS removes them and Nipun found them confusing). Slides: `eval-setup`, `tp-rule`, `eval-walk` (one box per click), `eval-fn` (FN, and why no TN), `pr-def`, `pr-threshold` (three cuts on the photo), the `pr-play` widget (score and IoU sliders; boxes marked TP/FP, missed dogs FN, the point on the PR curve), `pr-curve`, `ap-area`, `ap-code`, `map-iou` (AP at ten IoU thresholds on the same example), `map` (real per-class AP), `eval-code`, `iou-uses`, `recap-8`. The drawing helpers `tabs()` (non-overlapping labels) and `teachSVG()` live in widgets.js. The old real-data slides (street, bird, live AP on 38 person boxes, the 13-pixel pedestrian) are now optional in Appendix B. Every recap carries a `chart()` flowchart of its part (Parts 3 to 8; Part 6 has a new recap with the NMS loop); the lecture ends with `pipeline` (the whole lecture as one flowchart, with the part covering each step) and `summary` (the lecture in one slide), as he asked. The oriented-box slides moved into Part 7 (7.2); the six-jobs slides and `next` moved to Appendix B. The NMS slides say whose score each box has (the cell's dog score).
- Numbers on slides must make sense, not just be real (Nipun caught a negative object? score). `science.py` ends with range checks: scores, probabilities, IoUs, AP, precision and recall in [0, 1] (GIoU may be negative), losses not negative, every displayed box inside its photo (raw YOLOv8 and DETR boxes are clipped, as Ultralytics does). The toy network's last step squashes its outputs as YOLO does: sigmoid for object?, b_x, b_y and the classes, exp for b_w and b_h.
- Code slides walk one line per click with `codeSteps([[build, line], ...])`, beside a figure that shows what the line did (IoU, NMS and `detect` in Parts 3 and 6).
- Reveals inside an SVG must use `gat()` / `gonly()` (SVG groups). An HTML `at()` inside an SVG string breaks out of the SVG; `verify.mjs` flags this as "stray content after an SVG".
- Teaching style (Nipun's, see `dl-teaching/generative-modelling-ii/FEEDBACK_LOG.md`): one move per click, one running example with numbers after each equation, results derived not asserted, real model outputs over invented ones (invented values are labelled on the slide), a route map at the start of each part and a recap at the end. The November 2026 rebuild also borrows Andrew Ng's moves (target vector with "?", crop classifier → sliding windows → one conv pass, the centre cell) and Karpathy's (look at the data, print shapes, show failures, the whole detector in a few lines).
